First words, lessons 1 to 6

RAG retrieval augmented generation
Before the AI answers, look up the relevant part of your own documents and give it to the AI with the question.An open book exam instead of a closed book one.
LLM large language model
The AI that writes text, like ChatGPT or Claude. It learned from a huge amount of public text, so it has never read your company's documents.A very well read new hire on their first day.
Model
A program that learned its behaviour from examples instead of being written rule by rule. "The model" usually just means the AI.
Prompt
Everything you send to the AI: your question, plus any instructions or text you paste in with it. The whole message you type into the chat box.
Hallucination
When the AI says something false in a confident voice. It is not lying. It is producing text that sounds right without checking anything.Someone answering a quiz question they half remember, with total confidence.
Training
The learning phase, when the model reads a huge amount of text before anyone uses it. What it did not read during training, it does not know.
Next word prediction
How an LLM writes: look at all the text so far, pick a likely next word, add it, repeat. Your phone keyboard suggesting "tomorrow" after "see you", scaled up enormously.
Grounding
Giving the AI real source text to base its answer on, so the likeliest next word is the true one. This is what RAG does.
Retrieval
Looking something up. The R in RAG.Searching the handbook for the leave policy.
Generation
The AI writing its answer. The G in RAG.
Keyword search
Finding documents that contain the exact words you typed.Ctrl+F, but across thousands of files.
Stop words
Very common words like "the", "of" and "do" that appear almost everywhere, so matching them tells you nothing. Keyword search ignores them or gives them almost no weight.
Embedding
A list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a piece of text. Texts that mean similar things get similar lists, even if they share no words.A map coordinate for meaning: "dog" and "puppy" get pinned close together, "dog" and "invoice" far apart.
Vector
Just a list of numbers. An embedding is a vector.(3, 7) is a vector with two numbers. Real embeddings have a few hundred.
Dimensions
How many numbers are in the list."384 dimensions" means a list of 384 numbers.
Similarity
How close two embeddings are. Close numbers mean close meaning.
Cosine similarity
One common way to measure that closeness, using the angle between two arrows. 1 means pointing the same way (same meaning), 0 means unrelated.Two compass needles: what matters is which way they point, not how long they are.

Building one, lessons 7 to 12

Chunk
A small piece of a document, usually a paragraph or a few. We search chunks rather than whole documents so we can hand the AI just the useful part.One page torn out of a manual, instead of the whole manual.
Chunking
Cutting documents into chunks.
Overlap
Letting neighbouring chunks share a few sentences, so an answer sitting right on a cut line does not get split in half.
Context
The material you paste into the prompt next to the question. In RAG, the chunks you found.
Context window
The most text the AI can read in one go.A desk that only fits so many pages at once.
Token
The unit the AI counts text in. Roughly three quarters of a word. Prices and limits are given in tokens."Unbelievable" might be three tokens: un, believ, able.

Making it fast, lessons 13 to 16

Index
Something built in advance so that searching later is fast.The index at the back of a book.
Vector database
A database built to store embeddings and quickly find the ones closest to a given one. Pinecone, Weaviate and pgvector are examples.
Nearest neighbours
The stored items closest to your question. "Top 5 nearest neighbours" means the 5 closest.
Exact search also brute force
Comparing the question against every single stored item. Always right, too slow once you have millions.
Approximate search
Finding results that are almost certainly the closest, much faster than checking everything, at the cost of occasionally missing one.Asking for directions instead of reading the whole map.
HNSW
The most popular approximate search method. It links each item to its nearby items, then searches by hopping from neighbour to neighbour.Finding someone in a city by asking friends of friends.
Quantization
Storing numbers with less precision to save memory.Writing 3.1 instead of 3.14159.
BM25
The standard way to rank keyword search results. Counts word matches, gives rare words more weight, and goes easy on very long documents.
Hybrid search
Running keyword search and embedding search together and merging the two result lists.

Making it good, lessons 17 to 20

Reranker
A second, slower, more careful model that reorders a short list of search results.A senior reviewer checking the junior's picks.
Cross-encoder
A model that reads the question and one document together and scores how well they match. Accurate but slow, so it is used as a reranker on a short list.
Bi-encoder
A model that turns the question and each document into embeddings separately. Fast, because the documents can all be done in advance.
Recall
Of the things you should have found, how many you did find."Recall at 5 is 90 percent" means the right answer was in the top 5 for 90 out of 100 questions.
Latency
How long the user waits for an answer.
Golden set
A list of test questions where you already know the right answer, used to check whether the system works and whether a change made it better or worse.
